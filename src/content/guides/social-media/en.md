A very common question is whether social media platforms remove photo metadata for you. The honest answer is: **it depends.** Platforms, apps, and formats behave differently, and the rules change over time. This guide explains what actually happens so you can make your own decision instead of relying on assumptions.

## Do platforms automatically remove EXIF data?

Many major platforms remove EXIF metadata such as camera settings and GPS when they re-encode an image for their servers. Instagram, for example, has long stripped most EXIF and GPS data from uploaded photos. Facebook applies its own processing that typically removes this metadata too.

However, this is not universal:

- Some platforms and messaging services preserve metadata, or behave differently depending on the file type and size.
- Processing rules are not guaranteed and can change without an announcement.
- A platform may re-compress an image and add new metadata of its own.

So while "most big networks strip EXIF" is a reasonable general rule, it is not something you should rely on for sensitive photos.

## Why you should not rely on the platform

There are three good reasons to clean your own photos first:

1. **Inconsistency.** Behaviour differs by platform, file format, and over time, so "it worked once" is not a guarantee.
2. **The download trap.** Even if a network strips metadata from the version it shows, the original file you uploaded still contains it. If you or someone else downloads the source from your device, or the platform stores originals, the data is still there.
3. **Direct sharing.** Sending a photo directly to another person, over email or a messaging app, often skips platform re-encoding entirely — so your metadata travels intact.

## What usually survives on a platform

It is worth being precise about what is typically removed versus what may remain:

- **Usually removed:** camera make/model, lens, settings, and GPS coordinates, when a platform re-encodes your upload.
- **May be added:** platform identifiers, processing timestamps, and compression markers in the re-encoded file.
- **Often survives:** anything in formats that skip re-encoding, and of course the original file on your own device.

## The safest habit

Because you cannot control how every platform processes your photos, the safest routine is to make cleaning part of your workflow before you upload:

1. Load your photo into this tool — it runs in your browser with no upload.
2. Review the scan results to see what metadata was found.
3. Remove it and download a clean copy to post.

This way, whatever platform you use — or whoever you send the file to directly — the sensitive data is already gone from your copy before it leaves your hands.

## Understanding what you are sharing

Removing metadata does not prevent every kind of exposure: a platform can add its own data, and people could still recognise a location from the content of the image itself. But stripping EXIF and GPS before you share removes one of the most common and most personal leak points, for free, and on your own terms.
